\begin{frame}[t]{1. The problem: a phase oracle with few ancillas}
\small
A binary neural network (BNN) maps a finite input $z$ to an output. Let
\[
 p(z)=1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\text{the output violates a specified property}.
\]
A phase oracle marks these inputs without measuring them:
\[
 O_p\ket z=(-1)^{p(z)}\ket z,
 \qquad \sum_z\alpha_z\ket z\ \longmapsto\ \sum_z\alpha_z(-1)^{p(z)}\ket z.
\]
With $A$ clean ancillas, put $J_A\ket z=\ket z\ket0^{\otimes A}$. A unitary circuit obeys
\[
 U_CJ_A=J_AO_p.
\]
\takeaway{Reduce the largest number of ancillas needed at once. Also count T gates, CNOTs, total gates, and time. Every auxiliary qubit must return to $\ket0$.}
\src{Background: Nielsen--Chuang [NC]. The tested functions are structured feature-and-mask predicates, not complete BNN verification workloads [D].}
\end{frame}

% 2
\begin{frame}[t]{2. Amplitude amplification repeats the oracle}
\small
Start with $\ket\psi=2^{-n/2}\sum_z\ket z$. If $M$ inputs violate the property, write
\[
 \ket\psi=\cos\theta\ket{\mathrm{safe}}+\sin\theta\ket{\mathrm{violating}},
 \qquad \sin^2\theta=M/2^n.
\]
For $0<M<2^n$, these are normalized uniform sums over the two input sets.
\[
 Q=(2\ket\psi\bra\psi-I)O_p,
 \qquad \Pr[\mathrm{violation\ after\ }j\mathrm{\ steps}]
       =\sin^2((2j+1)\theta).
\]
\labeltext{Reason.} The oracle and reflection about $\ket\psi$ are two reflections. Their product rotates this two-dimensional state by $2\theta$.
\takeaway{Each step calls the oracle again. A smaller oracle reduces that repeated cost. An unsuccessful finite run is not, by itself, a proof that no violation exists.}
\src{Amplitude amplification: [NC], Ch. 6. Resource results below concern only the oracle; state preparation and the other reflection are excluded [D].}
\end{frame}

% 3
\begin{frame}[t]{3. A frontier record is a partial quantum circuit}
\small
The frontier $\mathcal F_t$ contains discovered candidates that can still be extended:
\[
 \boxed{v=(G_v,\Theta_v,\mathcal R_v,\phi_v,\rho_v).}
\]
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}ll@{}}
$G_v$ & Actual gates and their dependency graph.\\[2pt]
$\Theta_v$ & Clifford transformation, stored as a tableau.\\[2pt]
$\mathcal R_v$ & Ordered sequence of Pauli rotations.\\[2pt]
$\phi_v$ & Scalar phase needed for exact equality.\\[2pt]
$\rho_v$ & Consumed gates, per-wire depths, and other resources.
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\[
 U_v=e^{i\phi_v}C_vR_{P_m}(\theta_m)\cdots R_{P_1}(\theta_1)
 \quad\text{(rightmost acts first).}
\]
\takeaway{The RL state $S_t$ is the \emph{whole search situation}, including all alternatives and limits. Selecting one record $v$ is an action, not a choice of the whole RL state.}
\src{Native representation and frontier: model.py and native\_search.py [N]. A measured protocol also needs its classical outcomes and pending corrections.}
\end{frame}

% 4
\begin{frame}[t]{4. Tableau, rotations, and gate dependencies}
\small
\begin{columns}[T,onlytextwidth]
\begin{column}{0.48\textwidth}
\labeltext{Clifford tableau}
\[
 C X_j C^\dagger,\quad C Z_j C^\dagger
\]
are signed Pauli operators. These images specify how $C$ transforms every Pauli, up to a scalar phase.

\smallskip
$H$, $S$, and CNOT are Clifford gates. The separate $\phi_v$ retains the scalar phase.
\end{column}
\begin{column}{0.48\textwidth}
\labeltext{Ordered Pauli rotations}
\[
 R_P(\theta)=e^{-i\theta P/2},\qquad
 P=\pm P_1\otimes\cdots\otimes P_n.
\]
Each $P_j$ is $I,X,Y,$ or $Z$. For example,
\[
 T=e^{i\pi/8}R_Z(\pi/4).
\]
Noncommuting rotations cannot in general be exchanged. Their order matters.
\end{column}
\end{columns}
\smallskip
\labeltext{Dependency graph.} A gate on wires $Q_g$ has layer
\[
 d(g)=1+\max_{q\in Q_g}d_q,
 \qquad d_q\leftarrow d(g)\ \ (q\in Q_g).
\]
It records which gates may overlap. Exact rewrites can identify equal operators without pretending that their recorded gate costs are equal.
\src{Clifford/Pauli background: [NC]; scalar handling and dependency updates: [N]. Policies use compact summaries, not a graph neural network.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[t]{5. Reinforcement learning orders the search}
\small
A learner chooses an action, receives a reward, and improves its estimate of future reward. Here it controls a \emph{classical circuit search}, not a quantum device.
\[
 G_t=\sum_{k=t}^{T-1}R_{k+1},
 \qquad q^\pi(S,v)=\mathbb E_\pi[G_t\mid S_t=S,\ v_t=v].
\]
$T$ is the final search decision; the discount is $\gamma=1$.
\begin{center}
\begin{tikzpicture}[node distance=5mm,every node/.style={font=\small,align=center},box/.style={draw=accent,rounded corners=1mm,minimum height=10mm,text width=37mm},>=Stealth]
\node[box] (a) {SARSA selects a\\frontier record $v_t$};
\node[box,right=of a] (b) {LinUCB ranks its\\eligible continuations};
\node[box,right=of b] (c) {Exact transition,\\resource check, verification};
\draw[->] (a)--(b);\draw[->] (b)--(c);
\end{tikzpicture}
\end{center}
Rewards favor correct low-resource circuits and fewer search allocations; intermediate progress also supplies feedback.
\takeaway{Learning changes the order of exploration. It cannot accept an incorrect circuit or override a proved resource restriction.}
\src{Return and action value: Sutton--Barto [SB]. Native and cleanup controllers: [N, L]. Each search allocation may emit several physical gates; their full cost is counted.}
\end{frame}

% 6
\begin{frame}[t]{6. SARSA selects the next frontier record}
\small
Let $x_t=x(S_t,v_t)$ summarize progress, resources, and remaining work. Approximate the action value by
\[
 \widehat q_w(S_t,v_t)=w^{\mathsf T}x_t.
\]
The temporal-difference error uses the \emph{next record actually selected}:
\[
 \delta_t=R_{t+1}+w_t^{\mathsf T}x_{t+1}-w_t^{\mathsf T}x_t.
\]
At termination the next value is zero. The implemented update is
\[
 w_{t+1}=\Pi_{[-20,20]^d}\!\left(
 w_t+\frac{\alpha\,\delta_t x_t}{\max(1,\|x_t\|^2)}\right).
\]
$\alpha>0$ is the step size, $d$ the feature count; $\Pi$ clips the coefficients.
\takeaway{Positive $\delta_t$ means the return was better than predicted. During circuit-generation tests the coefficients are fixed. Training completion does not prove convergence.}
\src{SARSA: [SB]. Step normalization and bounds: native\_policy.py and cleanup/policy.py [N, L]; these safeguards are not a convergence theorem.}
\end{frame}

% 7
\begin{frame}[t]{7. LinUCB ranks the eligible next operations}
\small
For each operation family $f$, let $z$ be its context and $y$ its training feedback. A public prior $\beta_0$ is adjusted using
\[
 A_f=I+\sum zz^{\mathsf T},\qquad
 b_f=\sum z(y-z^{\mathsf T}\beta_0),\qquad
 \widehat\beta_f=\beta_0+A_f^{-1}b_f.
\]
Rank eligible operations by
\[
 \underbrace{z^{\mathsf T}\widehat\beta_f}_{\text{estimated value}}
 +\underbrace{\kappa\sqrt{z^{\mathsf T}A_f^{-1}z}}_{\text{training exploration}}.
\]
$\kappa\ge0$ controls exploration. The feedback uses reward and frontier-value information. Evaluation fixes the coefficients and turns exploration off.
\takeaway{Current oracle results use \textbf{deterministic algebraic constructions}. SARSA--LinUCB optionally searches full-bank schedules; it does not choose the factorization or exact lifetime order.}
\src{Current roles and disjoint linear models: [L, C]. A family is a native gate type or a permitted cleanup operation. No general contextual-bandit regret claim is made.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[t]{8. Measurement needs a phase correction}
\small
A temporary value is entangled with the input: $\sum_z\alpha_z\ket z\ket{f(z)}$.
With $\ket{s_X}=(\ket0+(-1)^s\ket1)/\sqrt2$, $s\in\{0,1\}$,
\[
 \bra{s_X}f(z)\rangle=2^{-1/2}(-1)^{sf(z)}.
\]
Apply the correction $D_f^s\ket z=(-1)^{sf(z)}\ket z$ and reset the measured qubit:
\[
 (-1)^{sf(z)}(-1)^{sf(z)}=1
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad K_sJ_A=2^{-1/2}J_AO_p.
\]
Any existing oracle sign $(-1)^{p(z)}$ is unchanged; the outcome probability is $1/2$.

\smallskip
\labeltext{Useful case:} for $f=uv$, the correction is $\CZ_{u,v}^{\,s}$. An established clean-target AND computes $uv$ with four T gates; this cleanup needs no more T gates.
\takeaway{Correction operands must remain available. A measured protocol needs coherent records \emph{plus} outcomes and correction dependencies; it is not one unitary tableau-and-rotation word.}
\src{Known mechanisms: Gidney [G], Amy--Ross [AR]. Exact local AND, reset, and branch checks: [D, N]. Four T gates implement a clean-target isometry, not a Toffoli on arbitrary target input.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[t]{9. The full-bank workspace theorem}
\small
Independent inputs: $a\in\F$, $b\in\F^r$, $x\in\F^m$; products $F_{ij}=ab_ix_j$.
All $rm$ products must coexist. The $k$ retained helpers satisfy
\[
 H=a(Pb\oplus Qx\oplus c).
\]
Process $F$ alone, then apply Clifford feedback preserving the live values. Finally measure $H$ in $X$ and correct on the original inputs. Require deterministic cleanup.
\[
 \boxed{A_{\mathrm{peak}}^{\mathrm{full\ bank}}=rm+\min(r,m).}
\]
\begin{columns}[T,onlytextwidth]
\begin{column}{.47\textwidth}
\labeltext{Construction.} For $m\le r$, retain $H_j=ax_j$ and compute $F_{ij}=b_iH_j$. Measure and correct $F$, then $H$. The case $r<m$ is symmetric.
\end{column}
\begin{column}{.50\textwidth}
\labeltext{Converse idea.} If $k<\min(r,m)$, kernels of $P,Q$ give at least $2^{m-k}>1$ garbage labels indistinguishable by the allowed feedback. Measurement completeness implies $P_{\rm success}\le2^{-(m-k)}<1$.
\end{column}
\end{columns}
\takeaway{The bound requires the full bank and this cleanup model. It is not a minimum ancilla count for every circuit implementing the final oracle.}
\src{Supplied theorem [H], pp. 53--60. NOTES.md gives the indistinguishable labels, the proof-only $h=ab$ register, and the reduction to erasing $F$ alone. Original inputs are excluded from $A_{\mathrm{peak}}$.}
\end{frame}

% 10
\begin{frame}[t]{10. Compute the phase, not the whole bank}
\small
The required phase is $p=q(a,b,x,F)$, where $q$ is quadratic in its displayed variables. Substitute $F_{ij}=ab_ix_j$ and simplify before construction:
\[
 p=q_0\oplus aB(b)^{\mathsf T}MX(x)
   =q_0\oplus\bigoplus_{\ell=1}^{d}aL_\ell(b)R_\ell(x).
\]
$q_0$ is quadratic; $B,X$ list linear/quadratic features; $d=\operatorname{rank}_{\F}M$.

For one factor, form $L,R$ and use a clean helper $t$:
\[
 \ket{a,L,R,0}\xrightarrow{\AND(a,L;t)}\ket{a,L,R,aL}
 \xrightarrow{\CZ(t,R)}(-1)^{aLR}\ket{a,L,R,aL}.
\]
Measure $t$ and correct with $\CZ(a,L)^s$, as on slide 8; then restore the parities.
For $s_f$ distinct stored quadratic products,
\[
 \boxed{N_T=4(d+s_f),\qquad A_{\mathrm{peak}}\le s_f+1.}
\]
\labeltext{Reason.} There are $s_f+d$ four-T ANDs and one reused factor helper. Rank minimizes factors only in the fixed feature basis, not T-count over all circuits.
\src{Exact construction: [C, D]. All parity formation, restoration, measurements and corrections are counted. Boolean relations describe this oracle family, not a replacement of the native hybrid representation.}
\end{frame}

% 11
\begin{frame}[t]{11. Prepare at first use; erase after last use}
\small
Fix the factors. Let $S_j$ be the temporary features used by factor $j$, and $A$ the set of completed factors. Without recomputation,
\[
 L(A)=\left(\bigcup_{i\in A}S_i\right)\cap
      \left(\bigcup_{i\notin A}S_i\right),\qquad
 w(A,j)=1+|L(A)\cup S_j|.
\]
The minimum peak within these orders is
\[
 V(A)=\min_{j\notin A}\max\{w(A,j),V(A\cup\{j\})\},
 \qquad V(\{1,\ldots,d\})=0.
\]
\labeltext{Why exact?} $L(A)$ must survive for future uses; $S_j$ must exist during $j$. Preparing at first use and erasing at last use attains each bound.

\smallskip
For $S_1=S_3=\{A,D\}$ and $S_2=S_4=\{B,C\}$:
\[
 (1,2,3,4):\ A_{\mathrm{peak}}=5
 \ \longrightarrow\ 
 (1,3,2,4):\ A_{\mathrm{peak}}=3,
 \qquad N_T=32\text{ in both}.
\]
\src{Quantum construction [C, D]; established minimum-open-stacks scheduling [MOS]. Factors are sequential, fixed, and not recomputed; features are not encoded together. The implementation uses exact ordering only for $d\le16$.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[t]{15. Conclusion: proven construction, optional learning}
\small
\labeltext{Proved.}\par
Every corrected branch gives the required phase and clean ancillas. The full-bank bound is tight within its architecture. Fixed-factor ordering is exact within its no-recomputation model.

\medskip
\labeltext{Observed.}\par
Exact algebra and reuse often lower clean workspace. The complete circuit may need more T gates or more measurement time than a competing construction.

\medskip
\labeltext{Role of reinforcement learning.}\par
SARSA selects frontier records; LinUCB ranks continuations. Both remain available, but the current resource gains are deterministic. A learned advantage is not established.
\takeaway{\textbf{Claim:} certified synthesis for a specified Boolean phase-oracle family, with workspace--T-count--latency trade-offs. Not global ancilla optimality, complete BNN-verification speedup, or SARSA--LinUCB superiority.}
\src{Next test: equal ancilla and latency caps, with scheduling and recomputation allowed on both sides. Full assumptions, references and short proofs are supplied in NOTES.md, not in additional slides.}
\end{frame}
