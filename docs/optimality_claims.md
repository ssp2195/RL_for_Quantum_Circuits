# Optimality claim contract

A circuit is called optimal only relative to a fully specified finite domain:

- target unitary or clean-ancilla isometry;
- native or macro gate library;
- logical and ancillary register contract;
- connectivity graph;
- exact or projective phase convention;
- permission or prohibition of measurements and reset;
- scalar, lexicographic, or Pareto objective.

The learned outer SARSA and inner LinUCB policies may determine the order in
which frontier records and pending continuations are examined. They do not
provide a lower bound. A machine-readable optimality certificate contains an
explicit circuit (upper bound) and a deterministic exhaustion or shortest-path
proof excluding every smaller resource value (lower bound).

## Certified domains

### BNN-like single-violation phase oracles

For three data qubits, the class

```text
O_s |x> = (-1)^[x=s] |x>
```

is reduced by Clifford X conjugation to CCZ. Exhaustion of all `8^7` linear
phase-polynomial coefficient vectors proves a minimum T count of seven in the
ancilla-free affine-input CNOT+T phase-polynomial normal form. A second BFS
over invertible parity configurations proves a minimum of six CNOTs and CNOT
depth six for realizing all seven required parities and restoring the identity
linear map.

These are conditional native claims: they do not cover measurement-assisted
or arbitrary ancillary implementations.

### Reversible BNN evaluator

Lexicographic Dijkstra search is performed over exact promised-input mappings
of the target-independent five-wire X/CNOT/Toffoli grammar. Costs are the
fixed native macro costs used by the oracle synthesizer. The six-macro learned
evaluator for the violation `100` attains the exact lexicographic optimum

```text
(macro count, T count, CNOT count, native gate count) = (6, 21, 21, 48)
```

in this declared domain.

### Four-qubit linear reversible gates

Breadth-first search over the complete group generated by all directed CNOTs
proves minimum CNOT count. A second BFS over all pairwise-disjoint CNOT layers
proves minimum CNOT depth. These claims are global inside the all-to-all,
measurement-free linear reversible model.

## Upper-bound-only domains

The restricted one-clean-ancilla QFT-3 construction is independently
certified, but this proof layer does not establish a matching lower bound.
Its resource figures must therefore be called certified upper bounds rather
than optima.

## Interpretation

The proof-producing architecture separates three tasks:

1. outer linear SARSA and inner linear LinUCB locate useful incumbents and order
   exact work;
2. deterministic canonicalization and resource-Pareto pruning remove only
   soundly redundant search states;
3. finite-state exhaustion supplies the lower certificate needed for an
   optimality statement.

Thus learning can reduce time to an incumbent or time to proof, but the word
`optimal` is justified only by deterministic agreement of lower and upper
bounds within the declared synthesis contract.
